# Student usability testing protocol

**Status: protocol only. No participants, results or feedback are claimed.** Recruit 2–3 consenting MSBA students. Test individually for 15–20 minutes. Explain that EchoPath is an independent prototype, examples are fictional in demo mode, participation is optional, and confidential workplace content should not be entered. Do not collect names unless necessary and explicitly agreed.

1. Explain the problem in one sentence without teaching the interface. Ask the participant what they expect EchoPath to do.
2. Ask them to find an analytics internship involving SQL, identify a challenge and explain one preparation lesson. Observe search, combined filters, and source recognition.
3. Ask them to contribute a nonconfidential or explicitly fictional practice experience, choose private storage or anonymous publication, review the public version, and submit. Ask where the original is saved and who can see each version. Time the task; do not count example autofill as a real intake measurement.
4. Ask them to build a four-week preparation plan using a selected experience, identify which advice came from that experience versus a general suggestion, and mark a task complete. Reload to verify progress.
5. Ask them to give feedback using the product dialog. Use usefulness and ease-of-finding ratings (1–5), plus confusing/frustrating moments and desired improvements. Ask whether they understood demo versus live AI.
6. Ask them to synthesize at least two matching experiences, explain the source count and cited advice, and use contextual Yes/No helpfulness. Observe two deliberate skill/keyword searches.
7. Ask them to save the private management link, edit their contribution, and withdraw it. Verify that the public copy disappears and ask what they believe happens to previously downloaded information.
8. Demonstrate opt-in reminder enrollment with a fictional address, eligibility, preview, simulated dispatch and opt-out/completion suppression. Confirm that they understand no email was sent.

Observe completion, time on task, navigation errors, unclear labels, evidence interpretation, privacy expectations and mobile usability. Record task observations using participant codes P1–P3. Ask before saving quotes. Store raw notes privately; export browser-local feedback only with participant awareness. Do not publish private feedback through the app.
